Radio Bar

A radio bar is a set of Radio options laid out in one line, inside a bordered track the height of an Input. Each option drops its circle and draws as a centered label, and the picked one sits on a slate-200 pill. The radios inside are the same component as everywhere else, so they keep their name, their value, their v-model, and the browser’s arrow keys.

<RadioBar>
  <Radio v-model="unit" name="unit" value="min" label="Min" />
  <Radio v-model="unit" name="unit" value="hr" label="Hr" />
  <Radio v-model="unit" name="unit" value="day" label="D" />
  <Radio v-model="unit" name="unit" value="week" label="Wk" />
</RadioBar>

The bar fills the width of its container, and the options space out evenly across it, with the same gap at the edges as between them. A bar next to an Input of the same size sits level with it.

Examples

Options

Every Radio inside the bar becomes an option. Its label is all that shows, so the description, the messages, and sitsOn on the radio itself are ignored inside a bar. Put error and hint on the bar instead.

Surface

A bar works out what it is sitting on for itself, by asking the Card or Navbar it’s inside. It takes the Input’s edge rather than the Radio’s, since the two sit side by side. The track is white on every surface, so the labels and the pill don’t change with it.

Size

The size prop on the bar sets the track and every option in it. The sizes are sm, md (the default), and lg, at 32, 40, and 48px tall, which are the Input’s sm, md, and lg. The labels run text-xs, text-sm, and text-base.

Error and hint

error takes the message to show under the bar. It recolors the track’s border, marks the line with a warning icon, sets aria-invalid on every option, and points each one’s aria-describedby at the message. Pass an array for more than one message.

Disabled

disabled on the bar fills the track slate-100 and drops the labels to slate-400. The picked option keeps its pill, with its label at slate-500, rather than losing the answer along with the control.

Interaction

Hovering an unpicked option fills its pill slate-100 and lifts its label to slate-700. The picked option does nothing on hover, since there is nothing left for it to do.

Picking an option slides the pill to it over 200ms, stretching or shrinking to the new label’s width on the way. Pressing shrinks the label to 96% for the length of the press. The shrink is motion-safe, so it doesn’t run for someone who has asked for reduced motion.

Focus draws a blue outline around the option’s cell, two pixels wide at a two pixel offset, and only for keyboard focus. Pressing it doesn’t ring it.

Everything else is the browser’s. A bar is one tab stop, landing on whichever option is picked. The arrow keys move through the set and pick as they go, wrapping at either end.

The labels and the messages clear AA.

The pill carries the answer, and at 1.2:1 against the white track it doesn’t clear on its own. The picked label steps from slate-500 to slate-800 alongside it, so the weight of the text moves as well as the fill behind it.

The disabled label runs slate-500 on slate-200 at 3.9:1. Disabled controls are exempt from the contrast rules, and it is deliberately quiet.
