Badge

A badge labels the thing next to it, such as a status, a count, or a category. It is a pill of text and nothing else. It doesn’t respond to hover and it isn’t clickable, so anything you can press is a Button, not a badge.

A badge sits on the label weight from Color: a 100 fill, a 900 label, a 300 border, and a 500 icon. Contrast has the numbers.

Examples

Color

The color prop sets the tint. The choices are sky (the default), teal, amber, rose, violet, and slate.

skytealamberrosevioletslate

The meanings come from Color. Teal is success or completion. Amber is caution. Rose is failing or destructive. Sky is the primary thing on the screen. Violet is an accent with no status attached. Slate has no meaning at all, and it should stay the answer for most badges. A colored badge in every row of a table spends the color budget on something nobody is reading.

A badge carries a fill and a border of its own, so it holds the same values on every surface. Unlike a Button, it never has to know what it is sitting on. Every surface a badge lands on is a light one. The dark card is the app navigation bar, and nothing in there is badged.

Extending the range

Seven more tints sit outside the palette. They are lime, orange, green, blue, fuchsia, pink, and emerald. They run on the same tint as everything else, so the treatment doesn’t change.

limeorangegreenbluefuchsiapinkemerald

Use them only where you need to extend the range of difference between badges. A chart legend whose keys are badges needs as many distinguishable values as the chart has series. A table column of tags, where a team has defined thirty of their own, needs the same thing. In both cases the job is telling one badge from the next, not saying what any of them means.

Everywhere else, use the accents. These seven carry no meaning, and next to an accent they borrow one they don’t have. Emerald beside teal reads as a second kind of success. Orange beside amber reads as a worse warning.

Reaching for them in order

Taken in this order, each color is as far from the ones already on screen as the set allows.

skytealamberrosevioletlimeorangeslategreenbluefuchsiapinkemerald

Past eight categories, reconsider whether color is carrying the distinction at all. A label, an icon, or a sorted order will do it better than a fourteenth green.

Size

The size prop controls height, padding, and text size. The sizes are 2xs, xs, sm, md (the default), and lg.

2xsxssmmdlg

xs is the size to reach for inside something dense, like a table row, a list item, or a card header. A taller pill there pushes the line around it apart. That’s most badges in the app. Drop to 2xs for a badge riding alongside small text. Reach for lg only when the badge is the subject rather than an annotation.

Icons

Icons go in the slot next to the label. They don’t inherit the label’s size. They are carefully sized for visual balance. Don’t guess, look at the component. They also take the 500 of the badge’s color rather than the 900 the label uses.

The 500 is decorative, at 1.9:1 to 3.7:1 on its own tint, so the icon can reinforce the label but never replace it. An icon with no label isn’t a badge. It’s an icon in a pill, and a plain icon would do the job better.
