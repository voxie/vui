Menu

A menu is a short list of actions behind one control. Press the control and a white panel drops under it holding a row per action. Pick one and the panel goes. Its main job is the overflow menu at the end of a list row, where three or four actions have to fit behind a single button.

The component is a wrapper and one sub-component. Menu holds the control in its default slot, positions the panel, and opens and closes it. MenuItem is a row in the panel.

<Menu placement="right">
  <template #default="{ open }">
    <Button outline aria-label="Actions" aria-haspopup="true" :aria-expanded="open">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-ellipsis" aria-hidden="true"></i>
    </Button>
  </template>

  <template #items>
    <MenuItem href="/campaigns/12/edit">
      Edit
      <i class="fa-solid fa-pen" aria-hidden="true"></i>
    </MenuItem>
    <MenuItem @click="duplicate">
      Duplicate
      <i class="fa-solid fa-copy" aria-hidden="true"></i>
    </MenuItem>
    <MenuItem disabled>
      Delete
      <i class="fa-solid fa-trash" aria-hidden="true"></i>
    </MenuItem>
  </template>
</Menu>

This is a menu of actions, not a way to pick a value. A control that sets a field is a Select.

Examples

The control

Whatever you put in the default slot is the control, and a press anywhere on it toggles the panel. Most of the time that’s a Button holding an ellipsis, with an aria-label since the icon says nothing on its own.

The menu can’t put anything inside your control, so two things are yours to set. The slot hands you open for both. aria-haspopup="true" says the button opens something, and :aria-expanded="open" says whether it’s showing. The explicit #default template is what lets the slot be destructured.

Items

Every row in the items slot is a MenuItem. Give it an href and it renders an <a>. Leave it off and it renders a <button>, and its @click is where the action goes. Either way a press closes the panel.

A row is text-xs, semibold, black on white, with 24px of padding at the sides and 16px above and below. Its content is a flex row with the ends pushed apart, so a label written first and an icon written last land at the two edges. Rows are divided by a slate-200 line, and the panel clips the first and last to its corners. The corners are the control’s own, read when the panel opens, so a panel under a sm button is rounded to 8px and one under an md button to 12px. A control with square corners gets 8px.

disabled fades a row to half and drops its pointer, so it neither presses nor closes the panel. A disabled row stays in the list so the reader can see the action exists.

The panel is a <ul> and each MenuItem renders its own <li>, so a screen reader announces how many rows there are. Anything in the slot that isn’t a MenuItem needs its own <li>.

Placement

The placement prop sets which edge of the control the panel lines up with. The choices are left (the default) and right. A menu at the end of a row wants right, so the panel grows back into the row rather than off the edge of the page.

The prop is a preference. A panel that would run off the right edge of the window lines up right whatever you asked for, and one that would run off the left edge lines up left.

In a list row

The shape the product uses most. A short outline button ends each row, and the panel lines up with its right edge.

Rendering at the end of body

The panel is positioned inside the wrapper, so an ancestor that clips its overflow clips the panel too. A scrolling table is the usual one. teleport renders the panel at the end of <body> instead, at the same spot on screen, where nothing can clip it.

Open the two menus below. The first is clipped by its box. The second has teleport and isn’t.

A teleported panel is placed from where the control is on screen and moves with it as the page scrolls. It’s still the menu’s child in Vue, so it still closes on a click outside, on Esc, and on a row being pressed.

Long menus

The panel grows to fit its rows until 16px short of the window edge, then scrolls. The example below holds seventeen rows.

A panel opening near the bottom of the window flips to open upward when there’s more room above the control than below it. It flips only then, so a panel that fits nowhere at least takes the larger space.

Disabled

disabled fades the whole thing to half and stops the control from opening it. Disable the button inside as well, so it doesn’t press and can’t be reached with Tab.

Interaction

The panel opens on click and closes on a second click of the control, on a click anywhere outside, on Esc, or on a row being pressed. Nothing happens on hover. A pointer crossing a list would open menus by accident, and a touchscreen has no hover at all.

It sits 8px under the control, or 8px above it when it has flipped, with a slate-200 border and a large shadow.

open is two-way. Bind it with v-model:open to drive the menu from outside, or read it to know when the panel is showing.

Every row is a real <button> or <a>, so once the panel is open its rows join the tab order. There are no arrow keys and no role="menu", deliberately. This is a disclosure, a button revealing a short list of controls, and Tab moves through everything. role="menu" would commit it to application-menu semantics and the keyboard contract that comes with them.

Closing with Esc hands focus back to the control that opened it, when focus was inside.

The labels clear AA with room to spare, at rest and in both states. The disabled row runs at 3.9:1, which is under AA for text. Disabled controls are exempt, and the row also loses its pointer, so the fade is never the only cue.

The divider is far under, and it isn’t asked to carry meaning. The rows are told apart by their padding and the panel is told apart from the page by its border and shadow, not by the line between rows.
