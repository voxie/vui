Switch

A switch is a <button role="switch"> that flips a boolean the moment it’s pressed. It has two forms. The plain pill is a thumb sliding along a track. The labeled form puts the name of each state inside the track, and the thumb sits behind whichever one is current. Both are the same component, so the props, the states, and the keyboard behavior are the same.

On the web, we almost always use the labeled version. The plain pill is reserved for the mobile app.

A switch has no label of its own beside it. Give it a name with aria-label, or point aria-labelledby at the text next to it. The words inside the labeled form are hidden from assistive technology, since read together they’d name the control “Draft Live”.

Examples

Plain and labeled

Left alone, a switch is a pill. Pass on and off and it grows into the labeled form, with the text for each state inside the track. The on and off slots take the same place when the text needs markup.

<Switch v-model="autoReply" aria-label="Auto-reply" />
<Switch v-model="live" off="Draft" on="Live" aria-label="Campaign state" />

The two label columns are always the same width, taken from the longer of the two, so the thumb covers exactly half the track in either state. The inactive label drops to 40% opacity and the active one sits at full strength on the white thumb.

Surface

A switch works out what it is sitting on for itself, by asking the Card or Navbar it’s inside. The on track is sky-400 on every surface. The off track is slate-300, and steps away from the surface where slate-300 would vanish into it. On white it lightens to slate-200. On sunken it goes to slate-400 at 50%. On dark it goes the other way, to slate-800, so the switch reads as a well in the panel rather than a lighter patch on it.

On dark the inactive label is white instead of slate-800 while the switch is off, since it sits on the slate-800 track. Once the switch is on, the track is sky-400 and the inactive label is slate-800 like everywhere else. The active label stays slate-800 on its white thumb.

The track has a shallow inset shadow and a 1px bottom border in white, so it reads as pressed into the surface. The border is white at 80% on default, sunken, and background, solid white on glass, and white at 25% on dark. On a white card it vanishes into the card, which is fine, since the inset shadow still does the work.

The page background is the surface nothing can announce. That’s what sitsOn is for. An element that paints a background of its own can wrap its contents in SurfaceProvider instead.

Size

The size prop sets the track, and in the labeled form the text and its padding too. The sizes are sm, md (the default), and lg. The pill is the height of a Checkbox box at each size. The labeled form is the height of a Button, so the two sit level in a toolbar.

Loading

loading pulses the whole switch, swaps the cursor for a progress cursor, sets aria-busy, and ignores presses until it’s cleared. The model isn’t touched, so the switch holds the old state while the new one is on its way.

 
<Switch :model-value="on" :loading="saving" @update:model-value="save" />

Disabled

disabled flattens the track to slate-200 and the thumb to slate-50, and drops the labels to slate-400. On dark the track is slate-800 at 50%, the thumb slate-500, and the labels slate-300. The thumb keeps its position, so a disabled switch still shows which way it’s set.

Interaction

Hovering deepens the track, sky-400 to sky-500 at 80% when on, slate-200 to slate-300 when off on white, slate-300 to slate-400 at 60% when off on glass and background, slate-400 at 50% to solid slate-400 when off on sunken, and slate-800 to slate-900 when off on dark, and stretches the thumb to 105% of its width. Pressing stretches it to 110%. Both stretches are motion-safe, so they don’t run for someone who has asked for reduced motion, and a disabled or loading switch doesn’t stretch at all.

The thumb takes 200ms to cross the track on an ease-out curve, quick and without a bounce, in line with Look and Feel. The label fades run 300ms.

Focus draws a blue outline around the track, two pixels wide at a two pixel offset, and only for keyboard focus. Pressing the switch doesn’t ring it.

Everything else is the browser’s. Tab reaches the switch, and Space and Enter both flip it, since it is a native button.

The active label clears AAA on its thumb, so the labeled form reads its state from the text.

The pill has only the thumb’s position and the track’s color to say which way it’s set. The sky-400 track against a white card is 2.2:1, and the slate-200 off track is 1.2:1, both short of the 3:1 WCAG asks of a control’s boundary. The thumb’s shadow is what lifts it off the track, since white on slate-200 is 1.2:1 on its own. On the dark surface the sky-400 track clears 3:1 and the slate-800 off track does not, at 1.4:1 against slate-700. There the white thumb carries the state instead, at 14.7:1 against its track.

The inactive label runs at about 2:1 against the track and the disabled label at 2.5:1. Both are deliberately quiet. The inactive label is a preview of the other state rather than the current one, and disabled controls are exempt from the contrast rules.
