We use Tailwind's palette as-is and call colors by their real names — `sky`,
`teal`, `slate`. There are no semantic aliases like `primary` or `danger` in
the code. A component takes `color="sky"`, not `color="primary"`, so what you
read in a class list is what you see on screen.

Meaning lives in the guidance below, not in the token name. That way a
button's color can change without a name that lies about it.

## Slate

Almost everything on screen is slate. It's the page, the panels, the text,
and most of the buttons. The other colors are exceptions you reach for
deliberately.

Slate reads as a ladder of surfaces stacked from lightest to darkest, with
the page sitting in the middle of the range so panels can rise above it and
sink below it:

### Neutrals step, they don't jump

A neutral element sits about one rung away from whatever is behind it. That
is the whole rule, and it's why [Button](/docs/components/button) takes a
`surface` prop — a slate button has no fixed value, only a distance from its
background.

| Behind it    | Neutral element | On hover  |
| ------------ | --------------- | --------- |
| white        | slate-100       | slate-200 |
| slate-50     | slate-200       | slate-300 |
| slate-300/30 | slate-300       | slate-400 |
| slate-200    | slate-300       | slate-400 |
| slate-700    | slate-600       | slate-500 |

Hover moves one more rung in the same direction — further from the
background, not toward it. On the dark surface that means moving *lighter*,
since darker would sink the element into the panel.

## Accent colors

Five accents, each pinned to one resting rung with a hover one step darker.
Sky rests at 300; teal, amber, rose, and violet rest at 100. No accent
appears at any other weight in a component, which is what keeps a screen with
four different colors on it from looking like four different products.

Sky is the odd one out because it's the only accent that appears directly on
the page background. At 300 it still separates from slate-200; anything
lighter goes muddy against it. The other four live on panels, and at 100 they
sit lighter than the page background — teal-100 on slate-200 is 1.09:1, and
rose-100 is 1.02:1, which is to say invisible. Keep them on a card.

Unlike slate, accents hold the same value on every surface. A sky button is
sky-300 on a white card and on the dark navigation bar. They're saturated
enough to separate from any neutral behind them, so they don't need to know
what they're sitting on.

## Text

Text sitting on a surface uses three colors, and only three.

<Card class="not-prose grid grid-cols-3 gap-6">
  <div class="h-24 rounded-lg bg-slate-800"></div>
  <div class="h-24 rounded-lg bg-slate-500"></div>
  <div class="h-24 rounded-lg bg-sky-700"></div>

  <div>
    <p class="font-extrabold">slate-800</p>
    <p class="mt-1 text-sm leading-snug text-slate-500">The default color for all text.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <p class="font-extrabold">slate-500</p>
    <p class="mt-1 text-sm leading-snug text-slate-500">For when body text needs contrast separation from a header or surrounding text.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <p class="font-extrabold">sky-700</p>
    <p class="mt-1 text-sm leading-snug text-slate-500">Marks text as a link inside body copy.</p>
  </div>
</Card>

slate-800 is the default — not black, and not slate-950. Those are reserved
for labels on a fill, where the text has a colored shape holding it. Body
copy on a surface sits at 800.

slate-500 is a step back for supporting copy, not a disabled state. It's the
one color here with a real limit: it clears AA on white (4.8:1) and on glass
(4.6:1), but on the sunken surface it drops to 3.7:1 and on the page
background to 3.9:1. On those two, use it only at large sizes, or move the
text onto a card.

sky-700 marks links. Note it's 700, not the light rungs the accents use — a
link has to be legible as text, which sky-300 can't manage. It clears AA
everywhere, down to 4.5:1 on the sunken surface. sky-600 is the tempting
alternative and it doesn't: 4.0:1 on white, under the 4.5:1 AA asks.

None of this needs setting by hand. `body` carries slate-800 in the base
layer and prose is pointed at all three, so text inherits the right color on
its own. Reach for a utility only to step something back to slate-500, or
where a surface flips the rule — the dark card sets its own light text.

### Labels on a fill

A label sitting on a fill is **black**. Not slate-800, which is for text on a
surface — a fill is a small, saturated shape holding a couple of words, and
it wants the maximum the ramp offers.

| Behind the label        | Label color |
| ----------------------- | ----------- |
| any accent at its rung  | black       |
| white, slate-50 to 300  | black       |
| slate-600, slate-700    | white       |

White on a light fill is never the answer — white on sky-300 lands at 1.67:1.

One exception, and it's the reason components need to know their surface: an
element with no fill of its own sits directly on the panel, so on the dark
surface it takes white. Black on slate-700 would be 2.0:1.

## Contrast

Every label pairing clears AAA, the narrowest being sky at 12.6:1 against the
7:1 AAA asks of body text.

Two columns, because the two models disagree in ways worth seeing. WCAG 2.x
is the ratio every audit and checker reports. APCA is the perceptual model
behind the WCAG 3 draft; its Lc is a score, not a ratio, and it produces no
pass/fail grade — roughly, Lc 75 suits body text, Lc 60 larger or heavier
text, Lc 45 headlines.

| Fill       | Label | WCAG 2.x | APCA   |
| ---------- | ----- | -------- | ------ |
| sky-300    | black | 12.6:1   | Lc 75  |
| teal-100   | black | 18.7:1   | Lc 98  |
| amber-100  | black | 18.9:1   | Lc 99  |
| rose-100   | black | 17.5:1   | Lc 94  |
| violet-100 | black | 17.7:1   | Lc 94  |
| slate-100  | black | 19.2:1   | Lc 100 |
| slate-300  | black | 14.2:1   | Lc 81  |
| slate-600  | white | 7.6:1    | Lc 91  |

Lightening the fill is what bought that. The four accents began at 300, where
rose and violet landed near Lc 63 — the weakest pairings in the system, even
though WCAG scored them above 8:1. WCAG 2.x is generous to very dark text on
saturated light fills and overstates what the last steps of the ramp buy;
lifting the fill was worth four times what darkening the label could.

Sky is now the floor at Lc 75, since it has to hold its rung to stay legible
on the page background.

The soft step between a neutral and its background is a different story. It
lands between 1.1:1 and 1.4:1 — a slate-100 button on a white card is 1.10:1.
That's deliberate and it's what makes the interface feel calm, but it's below
the 3:1 that WCAG asks for the boundary of a control. So the fill alone can't
be what tells someone a control is there:

- The label carries the contrast. Never build a neutral control whose meaning
  depends on noticing the fill.
- When a neutral control has to read as a control at a glance, give it
  `shadow` or `outline` for a real edge.
- Never signal state with a one-rung neutral shift. Nobody will see it.

## Naming

Use the actual Tailwind class. `bg-slate-900`, not `bg-primary`.

Semantic aliases go stale the moment a color changes, and they hide the one
thing you need when you're looking at a screen and something is off: which
color is actually on the element. If you need to express meaning, put it in
the component's prop — `<Button color="rose">` for a destructive action —
where the guidance above gives it a shared reading.
