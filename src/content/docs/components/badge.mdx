A badge labels the thing next to it, such as a status, a count, or a category. It is a pill of text and nothing else. It doesn't respond to hover and it isn't clickable, so anything you can press is a [Button](/docs/components/button), not a badge.

A badge sits on the label weight from [Color](/docs/foundations/color): a 100 fill, a 900 label, a 300 border, and a 500 icon. [Contrast](#contrast) has the numbers.

## Examples

### Color

The `color` prop sets the tint. The choices are `sky` (the default), `teal`, `amber`, `rose`, `violet`, and `slate`.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
    <Badge>sky</Badge>
    <Badge color="teal">teal</Badge>
    <Badge color="amber">amber</Badge>
    <Badge color="rose">rose</Badge>
    <Badge color="violet">violet</Badge>
    <Badge color="slate">slate</Badge>
  </div>
</Card>

The meanings come from [Color](/docs/foundations/color). Teal is success or completion. Amber is caution. Rose is failing or destructive. Sky is the primary thing on the screen. Violet is an accent with no status attached. Slate has no meaning at all, and it should stay the answer for most badges. A colored badge in every row of a table spends the color budget on something nobody is reading.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
    <Badge color="slate">Draft</Badge>
    <Badge color="slate">Scheduled</Badge>
    <Badge color="teal">Completed</Badge>
    <Badge color="amber">Paused</Badge>
    <Badge color="rose">Failed</Badge>
  </div>
</Card>

A badge carries a fill and a border of its own, so it holds the same values on every surface. Unlike a Button, it never has to know what it is sitting on. Every surface a badge lands on is a light one. The dark card is the app navigation bar, and nothing in there is badged.

### Extending the range

Seven more tints sit outside the palette. They are `lime`, `orange`, `green`, `blue`, `fuchsia`, `pink`, and `emerald`. They run on the same tint as everything else, so the treatment doesn't change.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
    <Badge color="lime">lime</Badge>
    <Badge color="orange">orange</Badge>
    <Badge color="green">green</Badge>
    <Badge color="blue">blue</Badge>
    <Badge color="fuchsia">fuchsia</Badge>
    <Badge color="pink">pink</Badge>
    <Badge color="emerald">emerald</Badge>
  </div>
</Card>

Use them only where you need to extend the range of difference between badges. A chart legend whose keys are badges needs as many distinguishable values as the chart has series. A table column of tags, where a team has defined thirty of their own, needs the same thing. In both cases the job is telling one badge from the next, not saying what any of them means.

Everywhere else, use the accents. These seven carry no meaning, and next to an accent they borrow one they don't have. Emerald beside teal reads as a second kind of success. Orange beside amber reads as a worse warning.

#### Reaching for them in order

Taken in this order, each color is as far from the ones already on screen as the set allows.

    <Badge size="xs" color="rose">rose</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="violet">violet</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="lime">lime</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="orange">orange</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="slate">slate</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="green">green</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="blue">blue</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="fuchsia">fuchsia</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="pink">pink</Badge>

Past eight categories, reconsider whether color is carrying the distinction at all. A label, an icon, or a sorted order will do it better than a fourteenth green.

### Size

The `size` prop controls height, padding, and text size. The sizes are `2xs`, `xs`, `sm`, `md` (the default), and `lg`.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
    <Badge size="2xs">2xs</Badge>
    <Badge size="xs">xs</Badge>
    <Badge size="sm">sm</Badge>
    <Badge size="md">md</Badge>
    <Badge size="lg">lg</Badge>
  </div>
</Card>

`xs` is the size to reach for inside something dense, like a table row, a list item, or a card header. A taller pill there pushes the line around it apart. That's most badges in the app. Drop to `2xs` for a badge riding alongside small text. Reach for `lg` only when the badge is the subject rather than an annotation.

### Icons

Icons go in the slot next to the label. They don't inherit the label's size. They are carefully sized for visual balance. Don't guess, look at the component. They also take the 500 of the badge's color rather than the 900 the label uses.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
    <Badge color="teal"><i class="fa-solid fa-circle-check"></i>Delivered</Badge>
    <Badge color="amber"><i class="fa-solid fa-clock"></i>Pending</Badge>
    <Badge color="rose"><i class="fa-solid fa-triangle-exclamation"></i>Failed</Badge>
    <Badge color="slate"><i class="fa-solid fa-tag"></i>VIP</Badge>
  </div>
</Card>

The same badge at every size:

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3 capitalize">
    <Badge size="2xs" color="teal">2xs Badge <i class="fa-solid fa-circle-check"></i></Badge>
    <Badge size="xs" color="teal">xs Badge <i class="fa-solid fa-circle-check"></i></Badge>
    <Badge size="sm" color="teal">sm Badge <i class="fa-solid fa-circle-check"></i></Badge>
    <Badge size="md" color="teal">md Badge <i class="fa-solid fa-circle-check"></i></Badge>
    <Badge size="lg" color="teal">lg Badge <i class="fa-solid fa-circle-check"></i></Badge>
  </div>
</Card>

The 500 is decorative, at 1.9:1 to 3.7:1 on its own tint, so the icon can reinforce the label but never replace it. An icon with no label isn't a badge. It's an icon in a pill, and a plain icon would do the job better.

## Contrast

Every color clears WCAG AAA and APCA's Lc 75 mark for body text.

| Fill        | Label       | WCAG 2.x | APCA   |
| ----------- | ----------- | -------- | ------ |
| sky-100     | sky-900     | 8.2:1    | Lc 82  |
| teal-100    | teal-900    | 8.4:1    | Lc 83  |
| amber-100   | amber-900   | 8.2:1    | Lc 83  |
| rose-100    | rose-900    | 8.0:1    | Lc 78  |
| violet-100  | violet-900  | 9.3:1    | Lc 83  |
| slate-100   | slate-900   | 16.3:1   | Lc 98  |

Badge text runs 10–12px, the size at which a contrast shortfall counts most, so the margin matters. [Color](/docs/foundations/color#contrast) has the comparison with the control rung.

The extended set lands in the same band:

| Fill        | Label        | WCAG 2.x | APCA  |
| ----------- | ------------ | -------- | ----- |
| lime-100    | lime-900     | 8.0:1    | Lc 84 |
| orange-100  | orange-900   | 8.2:1    | Lc 82 |
| green-100   | green-900    | 8.3:1    | Lc 84 |
| blue-100    | blue-900     | 8.5:1    | Lc 80 |
| fuchsia-100 | fuchsia-900  | 8.7:1    | Lc 82 |
| pink-100    | pink-900     | 8.2:1    | Lc 80 |

### What the tint costs

A 100 fill barely separates from the surface (rose-100 on slate-200 is 1.02:1), so the 300 border is part of the treatment and there is no option to drop it.

| Element     | On white | On the page background |
| ----------- | -------- | ---------------------- |
| sky-100     | 1.15:1   | 1.07:1                 |
| teal-100    | 1.13:1   | 1.09:1                 |
| amber-100   | 1.11:1   | 1.11:1                 |
| rose-100    | 1.20:1   | 1.02:1                 |
| violet-100  | 1.19:1   | 1.04:1                 |
| slate-100   | 1.10:1   | 1.12:1                 |
| sky-300     | 1.67:1   | 1.35:1                 |
| rose-300    | 1.92:1   | 1.56:1                 |
| slate-300   | 1.48:1   | 1.21:1                 |

Even with the border, nothing here reaches the 3:1 WCAG asks of a control's boundary, which is fine for something that isn't a control. The label carries the meaning. Never build a badge whose meaning depends on someone noticing the fill.
