A button is described by one prop. `color` names the fill. It works out what it is sitting on for itself, by asking the [Card](/docs/components/card) or [Navbar](/docs/components/navbar) it's inside. That matters because slate and transparent, the two neutrals, have to stay a step away from their background.

## Examples

### Color

The `color` prop sets the fill. The choices are `slate` (the default), `white`, `sky`, `teal`, `amber`, `rose`, `violet`, and `transparent`.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap gap-3">
    <Button>slate</Button>
    <Button color="white">white</Button>
    <Button color="sky">sky</Button>
    <Button color="teal">teal</Button>
    <Button color="amber">amber</Button>
    <Button color="rose">rose</Button>
    <Button color="violet">violet</Button>
    <Button color="transparent">transparent</Button>
  </div>
</Card>

Slate is the default because most buttons are ordinary actions that shouldn't compete for attention. Reach for a color only when the button earns it. Sky is the primary action on a screen. Rose is for something destructive. Teal, amber, and violet carry their usual meanings (success, caution, accent). White is the other neutral. Transparent is the quietest option, with no fill until you hover it.

White carries no border, so it needs something behind it. Use it on a colored or dark surface, or add `shadow` to lift it off a white one.

### Surface

A slate button steps about one level away from whatever it is sitting on. It stays readable without turning into a colored button.

| Sitting on   | Which is        | Slate button |
| ------------ | --------------- | ------------ |
| `default`    | white           | slate-100    |
| `glass`      | slate-50        | slate-200    |
| `sunken`     | slate-300/30    | slate-300    |
| `dark`       | slate-700       | slate-600    |
| `background` | slate-200       | slate-300    |

Nobody has to tell it which. A Card and a Navbar announce their surface to everything inside them, slots included, so every button below is passed nothing. The two surfaces missing from the table resolve to ones that are in it. `glassRaised` reads as `glass`, and `layered` as the default white.

<ButtonSurfacesExample />

The last row is the page itself, the one surface with no component to announce it. That's what `sitsOn` is for. An element that paints its own background can wrap its contents in [SurfaceProvider](/docs/components/card#a-surface-that-isnt-a-card) instead. A button with nothing above it assumes white, so a bare one on the page comes out slate-100 on slate-200, which is hard to see and easy to miss.

Everything else ignores the question. Sky, teal, amber, rose, violet, and white hold their value on every background. The five accents rest at 300, which separates them from any neutral behind them.

### Size

The `size` prop controls height, padding, and corner radius. The sizes are `xs`, `sm`, `md` (the default), `lg`, and `xl`.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap items-center gap-3">
    <Button size="xs">xs</Button>
    <Button size="sm">sm</Button>
    <Button size="md">md</Button>
    <Button size="lg">lg</Button>
    <Button size="xl">xl</Button>
  </div>
</Card>

### Outline

`outline` drops the fill and keeps the border. A faint wash of the fill comes back on hover. The label stays black, which reads on any light surface. On `dark` it switches to white, since black on the panel would be 2.0:1. With no fill of its own to read against, it's the shape that most depends on knowing where it is.

### Shadow

`shadow` adds a resting shadow that deepens on hover.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap gap-3">

### Loading and disabled

`loading` swaps the label for a spinner while keeping the button's width. `disabled` fades the button and blocks pointer events, for both button and link variants.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose flex flex-wrap gap-3">
    <Button loading>Saving</Button>
    <Button disabled>Disabled</Button>
    <Button disabled href="/docs/components/card">Disabled link</Button>
  </div>
</Card>

### Block

`block` makes the button fill the width of its container.

<Card>
  <div class="not-prose"><Button block>Block button</Button></div>
</Card>

### Link

Pass an `href` to render an `<a>` instead of a `<button>`. Styling is identical.

</Card>
