An input is the text field, and the textarea. Passing `rows` turns it into the other. A textarea grows with what's typed into it, with no prop to ask for that.

It follows [Select](/docs/components/select) for everything around the edge. A white fill, a slate-200 border on white and glass, and a shadow with no border on sunken, dark, and the page background. It works that out from the [Card](/docs/components/card) it's inside, or from `sitsOn` where there's no card to ask. Like a select, it belongs on a `glass` card or on the page background rather than on a white one.

An input fills the width it's given. Constrain it from the outside, with the column it sits in.

How a field goes together with its label, its description, and its messages is on the [Forms](/docs/patterns/forms) page.

## Examples

### Label and description

`label` is what the field is for, and every field in a form column gets one. `description` is a second line under it, for what the label can't hold. Both render above the control, and the label is wired to the field with `for`, so clicking it focuses.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose max-w-md">
    <Input
      label="Sender name"
      description="Shown at the start of every message from this group."
    />
  </div>
</Card>

Leaving `label` off is how a [compact field](/docs/patterns/forms#compact-fields) is written, and that's the only place it's right. Avoid `placeholder` everywhere else, since a field with grey text in it reads as a field that's already answered.

### Type

The `type` prop is passed through to the field. The choices are `text` (the default), `password`, `number`, `email`, `tel`, `url`, `search`, `time`, `date`, and `datetime-local`.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
    <Input label="Text" />
    <Input type="email" label="Email" />
    <Input type="tel" label="Phone" />
    <Input type="date" label="Send on" />
  </div>
</Card>

`number` and `password` do more than pass the type along. They have their own sections below.

### Surface

An input works out what it is sitting on for itself, by asking the [Card](/docs/components/card) or [Navbar](/docs/components/navbar) it's inside. The fill stays white. The edge follows the [Select](/docs/components/select#surface) rule, a slate-200 border on white and glass, and a shadow with no border on sunken, dark, and the page background.

<InputSurfacesExample />

Pass `sitsOn` on the page background, since nothing there can announce it. An element that paints a background of its own can wrap its contents in [SurfaceProvider](/docs/components/card#a-surface-that-isnt-a-card) instead.

### Size

The `size` prop sets height, padding, and text size. The sizes are `xs`, `sm`, `md` (the default), `lg`, and `xl`. They match [Button](/docs/components/button) and [Select](/docs/components/select) at every step.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose space-y-3">
    <Input size="xs" label="xs" />
    <Input size="sm" label="sm" />
    <Input size="md" label="md" />
    <Input size="lg" label="lg" />
    <Input size="xl" label="xl" />
  </div>
</Card>

On a textarea the size sets vertical padding instead of a height, since `rows` and the content are what decide how tall it is.

### Content inside the field

Three slots put things inside the field, and the value's padding is measured off whatever you put in them. `left` and `right` sit over the fill. `box` is a filled panel pinned to the start, for a unit or a prefix that is part of the value rather than a decoration.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose space-y-3">
    <Input label="Search contacts">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-magnifying-glass" slot="left"></i>
    </Input>
    <Input label="Budget">
      <span slot="box">$</span>
    </Input>
    <Input label="Message">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-face-smile" slot="right"></i>
    </Input>
  </div>
</Card>

`box` and `left` are mutually exclusive. When both are passed, the box wins and the left slot isn't rendered.

### More than one line

`rows` renders a textarea, at the height it names. Any value counts, so `rows={1}` is a field that starts at one line. From there it grows with its content and caps at half the viewport height, unless `maxHeight` says otherwise.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose space-y-3">
    <Input rows={1} label="Starts at one line" />
    <Input rows={3} label="Starts at three" />
  </div>
</Card>

At one row a textarea is exactly as tall as an input of the same size, and each row after that adds one line. The growing is `field-sizing: content`, so it's the browser doing it rather than anything measuring the content. There's nothing to turn on. Browsers without that property can't grow the field, so they get the drag handle instead. Where the property is supported there is no handle, since the field is already sizing itself.

### Numbers

`type="number"` runs as a text field underneath, so the value can be cleaned up as it's typed. Anything that isn't a number is dropped, the minus sign only counts at the start, and the decimals are held to whatever `step` implies. `min` and `max` clamp on blur, which is also when the value is padded out to the step's decimals.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
    <Input client:load type="number" min={0} max={10} label="0 to 10" />
    <Input client:load type="number" step={0.01} min={0} label="Two decimals" />
  </div>
</Card>

The stepper appears on focus and disappears again, so a row of numeric fields isn't a wall of arrows. <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> do the same job and are always there, which is why the buttons are out of the tab order. A `right` slot replaces the stepper.

### Passwords

`type="password"` with `togglePassword` adds the reveal button.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose">
    <Input client:load type="password" toggle-password label="Password" />
  </div>
</Card>

The button is in the tab order and it announces which state it will switch to, so the reveal is reachable without a mouse.

### Error

`error` takes one message or an array of them. Passing it recolors the border and the text, marks the line with a warning icon, and points `aria-describedby` at the messages.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose space-y-3">
    <Input error="Enter a valid mobile number" label="Phone" />
    <Input
      error={['Must be at least 8 characters', 'Must contain a number']}
      type="password"
      label="Password"
    />
  </div>
</Card>

There's an `error` slot too, for a message that needs markup, such as a link to whatever the reader has to go and fix.

### Hint

`hint` is the line under the field that is there before anything goes wrong. It sits at slate-600 behind an info mark, and stays put when an error appears above it.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose">
    <Input hint="Contacts see this name on every message you send." label="Sender name" />
  </div>
</Card>

### Counting characters

`counter` swaps the hint area for a [Char Counter](/docs/components/char-counter). It needs `maxlength` to count against. Passing `counter` stops `maxlength` and `minlength` reaching the field, so someone can type past the limit and see that they have, rather than finding the field silently refusing keystrokes.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose">
    <Input client:load counter maxlength={160} rows={2} label="Message" />
  </div>
</Card>

The counter is muted while the field is unfocused.

### Disabled

`disabled` swaps the white fill for slate-100 with slate-500 text.

<Card surface="glass">
  <div class="not-prose">
    <Input disabled label="Sending number" hint="Managed by your admin." />
  </div>
</Card>

## Interaction

Focus draws a blue outline, two pixels wide at a two pixel offset. Unlike a [Button](/docs/components/button), a text field takes the ring on a click as well as on a keyboard focus, because that's what `:focus-visible` does for a field you can type into.

On a shadowed surface, hover deepens the shadow from `shadow` to `shadow-md`. On white and glass there's no shadow, so hover does nothing.

The numeric stepper and the counter both key off focus. The stepper fades in, and the counter comes out of its muted state.

## Contrast

The values are the same as [Select](/docs/components/select#contrast), and so are the shortfalls. The border is short of the 3:1 WCAG asks of a control's boundary, and the message under the field is what carries an error rather than the rose border.

Three colors are the input's own. The label runs slate-800, at 14.6:1 on white and 11.9:1 on the page background. The hint runs slate-600, at 7.6:1 on white and 6.1:1 on the page background. The `box` is slate-600 on a slate-100 fill, at 6.9:1. All three clear AA on every surface.

The description is the one that doesn't. It runs slate-500, which is 4.8:1 on white and 4.6:1 on glass, and 3.9:1 on the page background. Description text is never large enough for the 3:1 threshold, so a field on the page background with a description is short of AA. Put fields that need a description on a `glass` card.
