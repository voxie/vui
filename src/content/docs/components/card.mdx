## Examples

### Surface

Content is passed as children, and the `surface`
prop sets the background: `default`, `glass`, `glassRaised`, `layered`,
`sunken`, or `dark`.

  </Card>
  <Card surface="glass">
    <h4 class="font-extrabold capitalize">glass</h4>
    <p class="leading-snug">Use this for content panels like settings, forms, or sidebars. It allows inputs to subtly standout from the background and lets us put a white highlight border on the panel.</p>
  </Card>

Only use on elements sitting on the page background, never nested inside other panels. This is because the panel transparency and color has been tuned to match the base surface glass color when mixed with the page background color.</p>

### Padding

The `padding` prop controls padding: `none` (no padding), `compact` (p-4),
`default` (p-6), or `spacious` (p-8). Use `none` when the card wraps content
that manages its own spacing, such as a table or an image.

<div class="not-prose space-y-8">
  <Card padding="none">none</Card>
  <Card padding="compact">
    <h4 class="font-extrabold capitalize">compact</h4>
    <p class="leading-snug">Used for sidebars, row items, or dense UI like a message inbox.</p>
  </Card>
  <Card>
  <h4 class="font-extrabold capitalize">default</h4>
    <p class="leading-snug">Used almost everywhere. Dashboard cards, analytics cards, form and settings cards.</p>
  </Card>
  <Card padding="spacious">
  <h4 class="font-extrabold capitalize">spacious</h4>
    <p class="leading-snug">Used for focused step by step flows like quick blast or modal content. Anything where the main UI is faded back or we have very low information density. Less stuff gives us more breathing room.</p>
  </Card>
</div>

### Element

The `as` prop sets the tag the card renders as. It defaults to `div`, which is
right for a card that is only a visual grouping. Switch to `section` when the
card is a titled region of the page, or `article` when its content would still
make sense pulled out on its own.

<div class="not-prose space-y-8">
  <Card as="section">
    <h4 class="font-extrabold">section</h4>
    <p class="leading-snug">A named region of a larger page, such as a settings group or a dashboard panel with a heading.</p>
  </Card>
  <Card as="article">
    <h4 class="font-extrabold">article</h4>
    <p class="leading-snug">Self-contained content, such as a message in a feed or a campaign summary in a list.</p>
  </Card>
</div>

Under the `layered` surface the tag lands on the card holding the content; the
offset layers behind it stay presentational.

## Interaction

Cards have no hover or active styles. Nothing about a card changes when you
point at it or press it, and that is deliberate rather than unfinished.

A card is a container, and its surface is the one thing it says: where this
panel sits in the stack. Those values are fixed by
[Color](/docs/foundations/color), so a hover state would have to move the card
off the rung it is documented at, contradicting the only message the surface
carries. A card is also large. Lighting a whole panel on hover puts a change
of state across half the screen without telling anyone what it does or where
to aim.

When a card is meant to link somewhere or perform an action, put a
[Button](/docs/components/button) inside it and let the button do that work.
Make it the card's primary button, so there is one obvious target instead of a
panel that might be clickable somewhere. The button is also the part that
takes keyboard focus and the part a screen reader announces as pressable,
neither of which a card can do.

<div class="not-prose">
  <Card>
    <h4 class="font-extrabold">Hubspot</h4>
    <p class="mt-1 leading-snug">Sync contacts and conversations with your Hubspot workspace.</p>
    <div class="mt-4"><Button color="sky">Connect</Button></div>
  </Card>
</div>

This holds for list rows too. A row of results is a stack of cards, and the
thing you click is the link or button inside the row, not the row itself.
