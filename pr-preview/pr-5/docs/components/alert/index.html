Alert

An alert is a message that sits in the page, for something the reader needs to know about the screen they are on. It fills the width of its container and stays until it is dismissed or its parent removes it. It has a role of alert, so a screen reader announces it when it appears.

An alert sits on the label weight from Color: a 100 fill, a 900 label, a 300 border, and a 500 icon. It is the same treatment a Badge gets, at a size that holds a sentence.

Examples

Color

The color prop sets the tint. The choices are sky (the default), teal, amber, rose, violet, and slate.

The meanings come from Color. Teal is success or completion. Amber is caution. Rose is failing or destructive. Sky is the primary thing on the screen. Violet is an accent with no status attached. Slate has no meaning at all.

Calm

calm swaps the tinted fill for slate-100 and the border for slate-200, and keeps the color on the text and icon. The text moves from the 900 to the 950 of the hue, since the 900 loses its tint on a slate fill. The message still reads as its color, with less of it on the page.

Surface

On a white or glass card the alert carries its 300 border and no shadow. On the page background, or anything slate-200 or darker, it takes a shadow and the border drops to the 50 of the fill. The shadow does the separating, so the border only has to finish the edge.

It works that out from the Card it’s inside. The page is the one surface with no component to announce it. That’s what sitsOn is for. An element that paints a background of its own can wrap its contents in SurfaceProvider instead.

Dismissing

The close button is on by default. Pressing it hides the alert and emits close. Bind v-model to bring the alert back, or to know from outside that it went. Left unbound, the alert tracks its own visibility.

Your changes were saved.

dismissible off removes the button. The alert then stays until its parent stops rendering it, for a message the reader has to act on rather than wave away.

Your trial ends in three days.

Actions

The actions slot sits between the message and the close button. It holds a Button or two. Only sm buttons go in an alert. A larger one makes the alert taller than the ones around it, and stacked alerts should read as one height.

The button takes the alert’s color, so the two read as one thing. The alert announces its fill to the slot the way a Card does, so a slate button on a slate alert steps up to slate-300 instead of vanishing into the fill.

Description

The description slot puts a body under the message. The message becomes the title, the icon and the buttons line up on it, and the description sits underneath in the 800 of the hue, starting where the title starts. Icon

The icon is a filled exclamation circle at the 500 of the alert’s color. The icon slot replaces it. The slot takes the same color, so an icon dropped in needs no class of its own.

The icon is decorative, so it needs aria-hidden. The words carry the meaning.

Interaction

An alert responds to nothing except its close button, which is a transparent Button at sm. It shows a fill on hover and answers Enter and Space like any button. There is no auto-dismiss. An alert that goes away on its own is a toast, and this system has none yet.

Contrast

Every color clears WCAG AAA and APCA’s Lc 75 mark for body text.

The 500 icon lands between 1.9:1 and 3.7:1 on its own tint, the same as a badge’s icon, so it reinforces the message and never replaces it. A 100 fill barely separates from the surface, so the 300 border is part of the treatment. Badge has the numbers for both.
